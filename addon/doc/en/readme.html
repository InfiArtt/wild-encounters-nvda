Wild Encounters for NVDA

A sweet, ambient Pokémon fan add-on, not a game. Now and then NVDA tells you which wild Pokémon is around you, from the real weather, time of day and moon at your home:

Psst! It's raining over Batam... a Wingull is gliding just above the rooftops near you.

No battles, no catching, no grinding. Every Pokémon you meet goes into your Field Journal. Each kind of Pokémon has its own little sound (water, electric, fire, leaves, wind, sparkles, shadows, earth), and a rare one has its own too.

Getting started

  1. Open NVDA's menu, Preferences, Settings, and choose the Wild Encounters category. (Or press NVDA+Alt+O, then O.)
  2. In New home, paste where you live: a map link, coordinates or your city's name. Then press Find this place (or Enter in that field). NVDA says what it found.
  3. Check Name said in encounters. It is filled in for you, and you can change it to your neighbourhood if you like.
  4. If you want Pokémon to come by themselves, check Let wild Pokémon come by themselves. Then press OK.

What you can paste as your home

  • A Google Maps link, including the short ones from the Share button (maps.app.goo.gl/...).
  • An OpenStreetMap, Apple Maps, Bing Maps or Waze link.
  • Coordinates, such as -6.2088, 106.8456, or 6°12'31.7"S 106°50'44.2"E.
  • A city's name, such as Batam. If there are several, add the country: Springfield, United States.

If a link doesn't say where the place is, NVDA tells you. Open the link in your browser, wait for the map, copy the address from the address bar and paste that instead.

Keys

Press NVDA+Alt+O, then one of these keys:

KeyWhat it does
WLook around for a wild Pokémon. You can do this every 20 minutes.
JOpen your Field Journal.
LSay your last encounter.
CSay how many Pokémon you've met.
SSay the status: your home, today's encounters, and when you can look around again.
OOpen the Wild Encounters settings.
HSay these keys.

Every command is also in NVDA's Input gestures dialog, under Wild Encounters, so you can give it a key of its own, or change NVDA+Alt+O.

Never too much

  • Looking around yourself works once every 20 minutes. Until then, NVDA only says how many minutes are left; nothing is asked of the internet. A try that fails (no internet) doesn't count.
  • Coming by themselves is off until you turn it on. Then the weather decides: when the rain starts or stops, a storm rolls in, fog settles, or at dawn and dusk, someone is much likelier to come by than on a plain sunny afternoon. At most once, twice or three times a day (you choose), never within an hour and a half of the last one, and never in your quiet hours (10 PM to 7 AM unless you change them).
  • A Pokémon that comes by itself waits until you pause, so it never talks over what NVDA is reading. If you stay busy for 20 minutes, it leaves.
  • Legendary and mythical Pokémon only appear on a full moon night or on your birthday (if you give it in the settings).

The Field Journal

A list of every Pokémon you've met, the most recent first. Press Enter on one to hear about it: its types, size, colour, where it likes to live, what it evolves from and into, and how rare it is. Copy puts a sentence to share on the clipboard ("I met a Psyduck in the rain today!"). Clear the journal forgets everyone, after asking. It is also in NVDA's menu, Tools, Wild Encounters Field Journal.

Privacy

  • Your home is kept only rounded to 2 decimals (about 1 kilometre), in the add-on's own folder in NVDA's settings (wildEncounters). The exact point you paste is never kept or sent.
  • When you press Find this place: a short Google link is followed on Google's own addresses only; a city's name is looked up with Open-Meteo's geocoding; the town's name for the rounded point is looked up with OpenStreetMap's Nominatim.
  • The weather comes from Open-Meteo for the rounded point: when you look around, and, while Pokémon may come by themselves, every half hour outside your quiet hours.
  • Pokémon names and facts come from PokéAPI. The requests carry nothing about you. Every answer is kept on your computer for 30 days, so PokéAPI is asked as little as possible (one request at a time, at most 300 a day, with a pause after a failure).
  • Your settings, your Field Journal and the day's count stay on your computer.
